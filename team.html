Background: how DRIs learn what they may decide

Question
How does a DRI find out what they're allowed to decide?
Why
If the rules were written in one place, an unanswered question could simply mean the DRI didn't look. Before measuring who gets answers, the review checks whether such a reference exists.

Context for the sections that follow. A DRI often needs an answer to two questions at the moment a client asks for something: who decides this, and am I allowed to do it myself.

Conclusions

  • No single written reference listing DRI permissions or decision owners was found in the reviewed sources.
  • Rules about DRI authority appeared as one-time posts, corrections after the fact, replies in threads, and remarks in meetings.
  • The one rule traced from start to finish was posted 89 days before it was needed, and no one cited it when it was.

Scope and method

Question
What was measured, and how?
Why
The tagging rules were set before the team comparison was run and were applied the same way to every DRI and every channel. Fixing the yardstick first keeps results from being shaped after the fact.

What was reviewed, and how each question was coded.

  • Period. July 31 to October 29, 2026 (90 days).
  • Sources. 25 Slack channels (19 state-client, 6 cross-team), with asks found in 24. In the cross-team channels, only asks made by or to DRIs were coded. Linear issues, email and meeting notes were consulted for individual threads. Direct messages and huddles were not reviewed.
  • Population. The nine DRIs on the DRI team roster, labeled DRI A to DRI I. The DRI manager is not in the population.
  • Unit. An ask is a written request for an answer or an action. Each ask was classed as ownership (who decides, who owns, sign-off, permission, commitment, assignment) or factual (information, logistics).
  • Outcome. Each ask was coded resolved, partial, redirected, acknowledged only, or no visible response. Resolved means the record shows the answer or action requested.
  • Totals. 318 asks by DRIs (187 ownership, 131 factual). 263 asks by other participants (126 ownership, 137 factual) serve as a reference group.
  • Statistics. Wilson 95% intervals; one-sided Fisher exact tests; permutation tests stratified by channel or by phase, 20,000 draws each.
  • Review. 91 threads addressed to engineering were re-read in a second pass, which changed six outcome codes. Figures use the corrected codes.

Conclusions

  • 318 questions from nine DRIs were tagged with the same definitions, alongside 263 from other participants as a reference group.
  • A second reading changed six codes. Those changes narrowed the gap between DRI H and the other DRIs; none of them widened it.

Ownership questions resolve less often than factual questions

Question
Do ownership questions get the same follow-through as factual ones, and how does each DRI compare?
Why
If every DRI's ownership questions stalled equally, the issue would be the type of question, not who asked it. Factual questions serve as a control, all nine DRIs are compared, and each DRI is removed in turn so no single person can drive the team average.

Across the nine DRIs, 58% of ownership questions were resolved (109 of 187), against 76% of factual questions (100 of 131). Ownership questions from other participants were resolved 73% of the time (92 of 126). The median first reply came after 0.83 hours for ownership questions and 0.2 hours for factual ones.

Conclusions

  • Across the team, 58% of ownership questions were resolved, against 76% of factual questions.
  • Ownership-question resolution ranged from 25% to 81% across individual DRIs.
  • DRI H's factual questions were resolved at the team rate, so DRI H's gap is specific to ownership questions.
  • DRI H's ownership rate (31%) was the lowest among DRIs with five or more such questions. The difference from the other DRIs combined (64%) is very unlikely to be chance (one-sided p < 0.001).
  • Removing any single other DRI leaves the others' combined rate between 57% and 66%.

Conclusions

  • Engineering received about half of all DRI ownership questions, in nearly equal shares for DRI H and the other DRIs.
  • Engineers replied to DRI H as often as to other DRIs, and sooner. This result does not support the hypothesis, and it is reported as found.
  • Replies to DRI H ended in a resolution less often: 33% against 64%.
  • Questions addressed to a group or channel had the lowest resolution of any recipient type.

Project phase

Question
Is the gap a side effect of working on newer states?
Why
Newer states have more open questions and fewer settled answers, and DRI H's portfolio leans toward them. This was the strongest alternative explanation, so it was tested directly.

States were classed by phase during the period. Three states that started in summer 2026, and one state in the month before its September launch, are early. All other active states are live. One dormant state and the six cross-team channels are excluded.

  • Five DRIs asked ownership questions in early-phase states; 16 of the 68 came from DRI H.
  • The only DRI assigned to a newly started state with no prior engagement (DRI I) resolved 1 of 4.
  • With askers shuffled within each phase, DRI H's difference from the other DRIs holds (one-sided p = 0.003, 20,000 draws).
  • Phase was assigned from the start dates DRI H reported for each state.

Conclusions

  • Early-phase and live states resolved ownership questions at about the same rate (57% against 61%).
  • DRI H's rate was lower than the other DRIs' in both early-phase and live states (31% against 65% in early-phase states, 38% against 65% in live states), and the difference holds when phase is held constant (p = 0.003).
  • Project phase does not explain the gap.

Decision rights in the written record

Question
Are the rules about what a DRI may decide written down and applied the same way each time?
Why
An unanswered ownership question could mean the DRI missed a rule that was available. This section checks what was written, when, and whether it was used when needed, including where DRI H missed it.

No single written list of the portal settings a DRI may change in production, with or without approval, was found in the reviewed sources. Statements of DRI authority appear as individual channel posts. Select an entry to read the record.

    1. The authority statement that applied on Oct 28 had been posted 89 days earlier. None of the Oct 28 responses referred to it.
    2. The same DRI acted without prior confirmation twice. The action on an engineering-owned field (issue status) was corrected. The action on a portal node setting was endorsed.

    Conclusions

    • The authority that applied on Oct 28 had been posted once, 89 days earlier, and no one cited it when the question was asked.
    • DRI H also did not find it, as recorded under DRI H's errors.
    • The same kind of action, taken without confirmation, was corrected once and endorsed once.
    • A request to a named person for a specific permission was answered in under a minute. A question about who holds a permission went unanswered for 29 hours.

    What resolution depended on

    Question
    What conditions went with a question being resolved?
    Why
    Looking at conditions rather than people points to what any DRI or manager could change. It also tests whether the gap is about being ignored or about follow-through.

    Five conditions in the record that went with a question being resolved or not.

    A response is not a resolution

    Of the 78 unresolved DRI ownership questions, 46 (59%) received some reply: an acknowledgment, a partial answer, or a redirect.

    In engineering-addressed threads, the named engineer replied to DRI H at the same rate as to the other DRIs (67% each) and sooner (median 0.37 hours against 0.69). The replies to DRI H ended in a resolution less often: 33% against 64%.

    A named recipient

    Named person: 104 of 174. Group or channel: 5 of 13.

    How work was returned

    In 9 of the 91 engineering-addressed threads, the work was redirected or returned to the asker. In one case, the return came with a five-step written guide and an offer to walk through it. In another, on Oct 29, the return ("you'd need to do the writing") came with no instructions.

    Escalation

    6 of the 91 engineering-addressed questions (7%) were re-raised to the DRI manager or leadership within seven days. Ownership questions addressed to the DRI manager directly were resolved 52% of the time (11 of 21).

    Conclusions

    • Most unresolved ownership questions received some reply, so the gap lies in follow-through more than in silence.
    • Questions addressed to a named person were resolved more often than those addressed to a group (60% against 38%).
    • In one matched pair, the same question was redirected when a DRI asked it and answered when the manager asked it. Rank, timing and wording differed between the two.

    Limits of the evidence

    Question
    What can't this review show?
    Why
    Stating the limits up front lets a reader weigh the findings at their actual strength.

    What this review can and cannot show.

    • Written record only. Direct messages, huddles and calls were not reviewed. A question resolved in those places appears here as unresolved.
    • Coding. Asks were coded by one reviewer with AI assistance. No second independent coder was used, and agreement between coders was not measured. The ownership and factual classes, and the split between decision requests and engineering work, involve judgment.
    • Sample size. Several DRIs have fewer than 15 ownership questions, and one has 4. Individual intervals are wide.
    • Phase. State start dates come from DRI H's account. One state's phase was corrected after an earlier draft; the corrected classification is used throughout.
    • Addressee. Each question is assigned to its first-named recipient. Questions with several recipients are counted once.
    • Cause. The record shows sequence and content. It does not show intent or cause.
    • Attributes. No personal attributes of the DRIs were recorded or analyzed.
    • Origin. This review was initiated by one of the nine DRIs in the population.

    Conclusions

    • The review shows patterns in the written record, not intent or cause.
    • Questions resolved in direct messages or calls appear here as unresolved.
    • One reviewer did the tagging, so a second, independent tagger would strengthen the results.
