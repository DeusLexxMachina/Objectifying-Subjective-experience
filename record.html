Does DRI H's question style explain the gap?

DRI H's questions are more often hedged, bundled and long. To check whether that style explains the gap, the comparison was repeated two ways.

  • Plain questions only (not hedged, not bundled, not self-deprecating): DRI A 86% resolved (31 of 36), DRI H 30% (3 of 10). One-sided p = 0.001.
  • Within DRI H's own questions: hedged questions were resolved 33% of the time (6 of 18), unhedged ones 29% (4 of 14).

Conclusions

  • In early-phase states, DRI A's ownership questions were resolved 78% of the time and DRI H's 31%. The difference is very unlikely to be chance (one-sided p = 0.003).
  • DRI H's factual questions, and questions directed to DRI H, were resolved more often than DRI A's, so the gap is specific to ownership questions.
  • The gap appears with every kind of recipient: engineering, the manager and other DRIs.
  • On in-office weekdays, the two DRIs were active on the same number of days, worked windows of similar length and posted their last message at about the same time. DRI H posted more and spent more time in active conversation. Differences in time worked do not explain the gap.
  • The two DRIs asked with the same courtesy and the same overall sentiment, and the replies they received were equally warm. Replies to DRI H were somewhat more often negative or returned the work, but not to a degree that is statistically clear.
  • DRI H's questions were more often hedged, bundled and long. That style does not explain the gap: plain questions from DRI H were resolved 30% of the time against 86% for DRI A, and hedging made no difference within DRI H's own questions. Adjustments 4 and 7 address the style regardless.
  • DRI H addressed more ownership questions to a group instead of a named person (5 against 1). That is a difference in DRI H's own approach, and adjustment 4 addresses it.

      Limits of this record

      Question
      What can't this record show?
      Why
      Stating the limits up front lets a reader weigh the findings at their actual strength.

      The limits of the team record apply here, plus these.

      • Selection. The two cases were selected because DRI H raised them. They show sequence and content, not frequency.
      • Matched pair. The same-question comparison is one instance. It shows a difference in outcome, not its cause.
      • Unrecorded events. The Oct 29 escalation to the manager was a live discussion and rests on DRI H's account; a 9:41 PM Slack post corroborates it.
      • Speakers. Meeting transcripts do not identify individual speakers on call audio. Spoken statements are attributed to "Ready-side" only.
      • Authorship. DRI H is the subject of this record and initiated its compilation. Selection of events was made with AI assistance and reviewed by DRI H.

      Conclusions

      • The two cases were selected because DRI H raised them, so they show sequence and content, not frequency.
      • The same-question comparison is a single instance and shows a difference in outcome, not its cause.
