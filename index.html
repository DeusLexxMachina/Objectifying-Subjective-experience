The question driving this exploration

Is there equal triage attention among peers?

Start hereTeam recordOne DRI's record (DRI H)

This textual analysis seeks to understand whether there is subtle bias, in effect if not in intent, in who gets answers and who is able to effectively self-serve with peers in public channels, as the Ready charter states. The corpus consists of questions about ownership and authority, like who makes a call, whether a DRI should or is cleared to act, or who owns a deliverable.

It covers written exchanges from July 31 to October 29, 2026, in 25 public Slack channels. Every question our nine DRIs asked in those channels was captured and tagged by type (ownership or factual) and by outcome (resolved, partially answered, redirected, acknowledged, or no response).

To ensure objectivity and to identify potential confounding variables, this analysis:

  • Compared all nine DRIs.
  • Compared like with like: same channels, same kinds of questions, same time window.
  • Removed each DRI one at a time to make sure no single person was skewing the team average.
  • Tested common alternative explanations, such as recently signed states, the type of request, and who the question went to, to identify how much of the gap each one accounts for.
  • Re-read every ownership question sent to engineering a second time for error correction. The corrections that came out of that pass lowered other DRIs' numbers, not mine.
  • Reported results that cut against my hypothesis, such as engineers replying to me as quickly as to anyone.
  • Included my own mistakes, alongside the information available to me at the time, so that I am accountable for my errors.
  • Recorded no personal attributes, such as gender, so none could factor into the comparison.

Results

There is a gap, and the data offer explanations for where it arises. My ownership questions were resolved about a third of the time, against about two thirds for my peers. Questions directed to me were resolved more often than for any other DRI. Project phase does not account for it: newer and established states resolve ownership questions at about the same rate, and the difference holds in both. A side-by-side with the peer who started a state at the same time as I did shows the same gap, with similar working hours and the same tone on both sides.

What the record points to is fixable: rules about what DRIs can decide live in scattered, one-time posts, and questions without a single named owner tend to stall. When the same question, or the same action, gets a different response depending on who's asking, delivery slows and confidence erodes on every side.
